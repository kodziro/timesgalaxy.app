Times tables tricks: one easy trick for every table

Last updated: 17 October 2026

Every times table from 1 to 12 has one simple trick that turns a hard fact into an easy one: for ×10 put a 0 after the number, ×9 is ×10 take away the number once, ×6 is ×5 plus the number once more. Learn the easy tables first, use the turn-around (3 × 8 = 8 × 3), and practise a few facts every day until the answer comes without the trick.

Which two rules cut the work in half?

  1. The turn-around. 7 × 8 and 8 × 7 are the same fact. So the 144 facts up to 12 × 12 are really 78 facts, and only 55 up to 10 × 10.
  2. Use the trick of the easier table. For 6 × 7, use the 6 trick, not the 7 trick: 5 × 7 = 35, plus 7 more = 42. For 6 × 2, just double 6.

What is the trick for each times table?

×1The 1 times table: Times 1 stays the same.1 × 8 = 8
×2The 2 times table: Double it: add the number to itself.2 × 7: 7 + 7 = 14
×3The 3 times table: Add the number three times.3 × 6: 6 + 6 + 6 = 18
×4The 4 times table: Double it, then double again.4 × 7: double 7 = 14, double again = 28
×5The 5 times table: Count by 5s.5 × 6: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30
×6The 6 times table: Times 5, plus the number once more.6 × 7: 5 × 7 = 35, plus 7 more = 42
×7The 7 times table: Times 5 plus times 2.7 × 7: 5 × 7 = 35, plus 2 × 7 = 14, together 49
×8The 8 times table: Double three times.8 × 7: 14, 28, 56
×9The 9 times table: Times 10, then take the number away once.9 × 7: 10 × 7 = 70, take away 7 = 63
×10The 10 times table: Put a 0 after the number.10 × 6 = 60
×11The 11 times table: Up to 9: write the number twice. After that: times 10, plus the number.11 × 4 = 44 · 11 × 12: 120 + 12 = 132
×12The 12 times table: Times 10 plus times 2.12 × 12: 10 × 12 = 120, plus 2 × 12 = 24, together 144

In which order should my child learn the tables?

Easy tables first, because each new table leans on one before it: ×4 is double ×2, ×8 is double ×4, ×6 is ×5 plus one more, and ×9 is ×10 minus one. Times Galaxy uses this order of planets:

×1, ×2, ×10, ×5, ×3, ×4, ×6, ×9, ×8, ×7, ×11, ×12.

By the time your child reaches the 7 times table, almost all of it is already known from the other tables: up to 10 × 10, only 7 × 7 is new.

Are tricks enough?

No. A trick is a bridge, not the goal. It helps your child find the answer while a fact is new. After that, short practice on later days turns the fact into quick recall, so the answer comes in 2–3 seconds without the trick. Our 5-minutes-a-day guide shows how.

How does Times Galaxy show the tricks?

Questions

What is the easiest times table trick?

The 10 times table: put a 0 after the number, so 10 × 6 = 60. The 1 times table is even easier: times 1 stays the same.

What is the trick for the 9 times table?

Times 10, then take the number away once. For 9 × 6: 10 × 6 = 60, take away 6 = 54.

What is the trick for the 7 times table?

Times 5 plus times 2. For 7 × 7: 5 × 7 = 35, plus 2 × 7 = 14, together 49. If the other tables are learned first, 7 × 7 is the only new fact up to 10 × 10.

What is the trick for the 8 times table?

Double three times. For 8 × 7: 14, 28, 56.

Which times tables are the hardest?

Many children find the 6, 7, 8 and 12 times tables hardest, for example 7 × 8 = 56 and 6 × 8 = 48. Learn them last, when the easier tables can help.

Should children learn tricks or just remember the answers?

Both. The trick helps while a fact is new. Practice on later days then makes the answer quick, so the child does not need the trick anymore.
